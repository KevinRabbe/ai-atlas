# Development and Genomic Encoding

## Required function

Generate a complex adaptive computational structure from a compact inherited specification plus environmental interaction, rather than explicitly enumerating the mature system.

## Evidence

- **B-S016 — Camp et al. (2019):** human, chimpanzee and macaque cerebral organoids showed reproducible developmental trajectories and species-specific gene-regulatory timing/accessibility differences.
- **B-S017 — Gordon et al. / long-term cortical organoid work (2021):** cortical organoids progressed through key maturation programs over long culture times, supporting substantial intrinsic developmental dynamics.
- **B-S018 — Fleck et al. (2023):** single-cell multi-omics plus perturbation reconstructed gene-regulatory networks controlling regional/cell-fate decisions in human brain organoids; perturbing regulatory factors changed developmental outcomes.

## Important inference

The genome does not contain a literal connection-by-connection description of the adult brain. Complex tissue organization emerges from regulatory rules, local interactions, gradients, cell-state transitions, growth and activity-dependent refinement.

**Classification:** inference, not direct one-to-one computational equivalence.

## Clean-sheet hypothesis

For sufficiently complex AI systems, it may be cheaper or more evolvable to encode a **developmental/generative program** that constructs and specializes modules than to hand-specify or directly optimize every final connection.

Possible artificial analogues:

- learned architecture generators;
- growth/pruning rules;
- local module differentiation from shared initial programs;
- developmental curricula that unlock capabilities/states in stages;
- compact genotype-like descriptions expanded into large phenotypes.

## What is not established

Development is slow, noisy and constrained by biology. It is not evidence that artificial systems should mimic embryogenesis or DNA. The actionable idea is indirect/generative specification and staged differentiation.
