# Evolution and Regulatory Change

## Required function

Understand how complex cognitive substrates can change across generations without redesigning every component from scratch.

## Evidence

- **B-S019 — Kozlenkov et al. / Nature (2023):** comparative single-cell regulatory analyses across human, chimpanzee and macaque cortex identified human-specific cell-type and regulatory changes, including many accelerated genomic regions associated with accessibility changes.
- **B-S020 — Won et al. (2019):** human-evolved regulatory elements were linked to genes and developmental programs involved in cortical expansion and neurodevelopment.
- **B-S021 — Wei et al. / Nature (2025):** CRISPR perturbation of selected human accelerated regions in human/chimpanzee-derived neurons demonstrated species-specific cis-regulatory functions for several regions.
- **B-S022 — HARE5 functional study (2025):** replacing regulatory sequence in model systems altered progenitor proliferation, cortical development and connectivity, providing a causal example of regulatory-sequence effects on neural phenotype.
- **B-S023 — Li et al. (2026):** comparative work on mammalian neocortex maps adaptive evolution in gene-regulatory networks associated with neuronal subtype diversification.

## What seems useful

Evolutionary novelty does not require inventing an entirely new computational primitive each time. Changing **when, where and how strongly conserved mechanisms are expressed** can produce major phenotype changes.

That suggests a software/AI analogue:

`shared primitives + evolvable regulatory/control program -> heterogeneous mature system`

rather than independently designing every specialist.

## Strong caution

Human-specific traits are polygenic, developmental and environmentally entangled. Individual accelerated regions are not "intelligence genes." The Atlas records regulatory evolution as a mechanism for changing developmental programs, not as a shortcut to explaining human cognition.

## Clean-sheet questions

- Can architecture search operate more effectively on compact regulatory/generative programs than on full model graphs?
- Can module specialization emerge through local developmental signals and selection rather than explicit role labels?
- Does indirect encoding improve evolvability, transfer and robustness under fixed search compute?
